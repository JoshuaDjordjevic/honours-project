# Crystal Rock Group Company Documentation

# 1. Overview

Crystal Rock Group (CRG) is a company focused on the collection and extraction of rare earth minerals from deep wells in Lithuiania. The company develops and operates specialised extraction equipment, geological monitoring instruments and mineral processing systems.

CRG operates three primary departments: Extraction operations, Instrumentation and Engineering, and Mineral Processing. Each department is responsible for a distinct stage of the extraction and processing workflow.

The company maintains internal documentation covering employee guidelines, equipment operation, maintenance procedures, safety requirements, and mineral handling. Employees are expected to follow the procedures outlined in this documentation when performing operational tasks.

# 2. Company Structure

CRG is divided into the following departments:

## 2.1. Extraction Operations

The Extraction Operations department of CRG focuses on operating extraction wells and managing mineral collection activities. Additionally responsible for reporting well performance and extraction volumes.

## 2.2. Instrumentation and Engineering

The Instrumentation and Engineering department of CRG is responsible for maintaining extraction equipment, sensors, and monitoring systems. Additionally responsible for maintaining service records.

## 2.3. Mineral Processing

The Mineral Processing department of CRG is responsible for separating and preparing extracted materials for storage and transport, and recording quantity and classification of recovered minerals.

## 2.4. Administration and Compliance

The Administration and Compliance department of CRG is responsible for managing employee records, operating documentation, and internal compliance procedures.

# 3. Employee Guidelines

## 3.1 Working Hours

Standard administrative working hours are 08:30 to 16:30, Monday to Friday. Employees assigned to extraction operations may work rotating shifts according to the operational schedule.

Employees must record the start and end of each shift using the company's internal attendance system. Missed attendance records must be reported to the relevant department supervisor.

## 3.2 Training Requirements

Employees must complete site induction training before entering an active extraction facility. Personnel operating specialised extraction equipment must also complete equipment-specific training and receive authorisation for the relevant device.

Training completion is recorded in the internal employee training register.

## 3.3 Incident Reporting

Equipment failures, unexpected pressure changes, chemical spills and other operational incidents must be reported to the shift supervisor as soon as practicable.

Incident reports must include the location, time, equipment involved and a description of the observed issue. Employees must not resume an affected operation until the responsible supervisor has authorised a restart.

## 3.4 Leave and Reimbursement

Employee leave requests are submitted through the internal administration system and require approval from the employee's department supervisor.

This document does not specify annual leave entitlements, salary bands or the maximum reimbursement for business travel. Employees should consult the relevant employment agreement or administrative policy for those details.

# 4. Device Usage

## 4.1. Deep Well Extraction Unit - DW-4

The DW-4 is a specialised extraction unit designed to collect mineral-bearing material from deep geological wells.

| Specification | Value |
|-|-|
| Device identifier | DW-4 |
| Primary function | Deep well mineral extraction |
| Maximum operating depth | 1,200 metres |
| Nominal extraction rate | 18kg per hour |
| Operating temperature range | 5c to 40c |
| Required inspection interval | Every 250 operating hours |

The nominal extraction rate is a reference operating value and may vary according to geological conditions and the composition of the material being extracted.

Operators must inspect the unit before each shift. Inspections must include the extraction cable, pressure monitoring system, electrical connections and emergency shutdown mechanism.

## 4.2 Geological Monitoring Sensor - GMS-2

The GMS-2 is used to monitor environmental and operational conditions inside extraction wells.

| Specification | Value |
|-|-|
| Device identifier | GMS-2 |
| Primary function | Well condition monitoring |
| Sampling interval | 10 seconds |
| Internal storage capacity | 16 GB |
| Data export format | CSV |
| Required calibration interval | Every 90 days |

The GMS-2 records pressure, temperature and vibration measurements. Data can be exported to the internal analysis system for inspection by the Instrumentation and Engineering department.

The device's documentation does not specify its wireless communication range or waterproofing certification.

## 4.3 Device Authorisation

Only personnel with current equipment-specific training may operate the DW-4 extraction unit. The GMS-2 may be installed and configured by authorised instrumentation technicians.

Employees must not modify device firmware, alter safety limits or bypass interlocks without written engineering approval.

# 5. Maintenance Procedures

## 5.1 DW-4 Maintenance

The DW-4 must undergo a scheduled inspection after every 250 operating hours. The inspection includes examination of the extraction cable, drive assembly, pressure monitoring system and emergency shutdown mechanism.

Any damaged component must be recorded in the maintenance register. The unit must remain out of service if a fault affects its safe operation.

## 5.2 GMS-2 Calibration

The GMS-2 must be calibrated every 90 days. Calibration records must include the device identifier, calibration date, technician identifier and measured deviation from the reference instrument.

A sensor that fails calibration must be removed from operational use until it has been recalibrated successfully.

## 5.3 Maintenance Records

All maintenance activities must be recorded in the internal maintenance register. Each record must identify the equipment, work performed, date of service and person responsible.

This document does not specify the expected service life of either device or the cost of replacement components.

# 6. Safety and Environmental Procedures

## 6.1 Operational Safety

Employees must complete the required site induction and wear the protective equipment specified for their work area.

Before operating extraction equipment, personnel must verify that the designated work area is clear and that required safety systems are operational.

## 6.2 Emergency Shutdown

The DW-4 includes an emergency shutdown mechanism intended to stop extraction activity when an unsafe operating condition is identified.

Operators must follow the site-specific emergency procedure when a shutdown is required. The equipment must not be restarted until the fault has been investigated and an authorised supervisor has approved the restart.

## 6.3 Environmental Monitoring

Extraction Operations must record significant changes in well conditions and report unexpected measurements to Instrumentation and Engineering.

CRG maintains operational records for environmental monitoring. This document does not specify the company's annual environmental impact, total mineral reserves or regulatory permit numbers.

# 7. Mineral Handling and Processing

## 7.1 Material Classification

Recovered materials are assigned an internal batch identifier when transferred to Mineral Processing.

Each batch record must include the extraction well identifier, collection date, recorded mass and preliminary material classification.

## 7.2 Batch Records

Batch records are maintained in the mineral processing register. The register allows authorised personnel to trace material from its original extraction well through subsequent processing stages.

The internal batch identifier must remain associated with the material throughout storage and transfer.

## 7.3 Production Reporting

Extraction Operations submits daily production records to Mineral Processing. Reports include the operating hours of each extraction unit and the mass of material collected during the reporting period.

The company does not publish a consolidated annual production figure in this document.

# 8. Internal Systems and Data Management

## 8.1 Operational Database

CRG uses an internal operational database to store equipment records, extraction logs, maintenance histories and mineral batch information.

Each device is identified by a unique device identifier. Employees must verify the identifier before creating or updating an equipment record.

## 8.2 Data Export

GMS-2 measurements can be exported in CSV format. Exported files must retain the original timestamps and device identifiers to support subsequent analysis.

## 8.3 Information Access

Access to operational records is restricted according to departmental responsibilities. Employees requiring access outside their normal responsibilities must obtain approval from the relevant department supervisor.

This document does not specify the name of the database software, the server location or the company's data retention period.

# 9. Glossary

- CRG: Crystal Rock Group, the organisation described in this document.
- DW-4: Deep Well Extraction Unit, used for mineral extraction.
- GMS-2: Geological Monitoring Sensor, used to collect well condition measurements.
- Operating depth: The depth at which an extraction unit is deployed within a geological well.
- Nominal extraction rate: The reference mass of material collected per unit of operating time under specified operating conditions.
- Calibration interval: The period between scheduled checks of an instrument against a reference standard.
